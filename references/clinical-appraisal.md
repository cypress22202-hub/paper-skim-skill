# Clinical appraisal criteria

Apply the relevant branch rather than mechanically answering every prompt. Separate fatal threats to validity from limitations that only narrow interpretation.

## Shared extraction

Establish the PICO/PECO elements, setting, recruitment period, inclusion and exclusion criteria, intervention/exposure and comparator, follow-up, prespecified primary and secondary endpoints, estimand or analysis population, sample-size rationale, missing-data handling, effect measures with confidence intervals, multiplicity control, safety collection, funding, author conflicts, registration, protocol availability, and deviations from the registered plan.

Check whether the population, comparator, endpoint, follow-up, and care setting match the clinical or product question. Prefer effect sizes, confidence intervals, absolute risks, and clinically meaningful thresholds over p-values alone.

## Randomized controlled trials

Assess random-sequence generation, allocation concealment, baseline balance, masking of participants/investigators/outcome assessors, contamination and co-intervention, adherence and crossover, attrition, intention-to-treat versus per-protocol handling, missing data, outcome ascertainment, multiplicity, selective reporting, early stopping, and sponsor influence.

For noninferiority studies, inspect the justification and clinical acceptability of the margin, constancy and assay-sensitivity assumptions, both ITT and per-protocol results, and whether the conclusion is actually noninferiority rather than equivalence or superiority.

For subgroup findings, check prespecification, interaction tests, multiplicity, sample size, consistency, and biological plausibility. Treat post-hoc subgroups as hypothesis-generating.

## Observational and real-world evidence studies

Map the target population, index date, eligibility window, exposure assignment, comparator selection, time zero, follow-up, outcome definition, and censoring. Look specifically for confounding by indication, channeling, immortal-time bias, prevalent-user bias, selection bias, informative censoring, surveillance bias, exposure or outcome misclassification, missingness, treatment switching, and database fitness.

Assess whether confounders were measured before exposure and whether adjustment was adequate. Examine propensity-score construction and diagnostics, overlap and positivity, covariate balance after adjustment, model specification, sensitivity analyses, negative or positive controls, quantitative bias analysis, and robustness to alternative definitions. Statistical adjustment cannot repair unmeasured confounding by assertion alone.

## Clinical meaning and applicability

Determine whether statistically significant results are clinically meaningful. Inspect absolute effect, number needed to treat or harm when derivable, baseline risk, durability, patient-reported outcomes, functional vision or quality-of-life relevance, safety tradeoffs, treatment burden, adherence, and feasibility.

Assess external validity across age, disease stage, comorbidity, prior treatment, geographic and ethnic representation, real-world practice, dosing, formulation, route, and follow-up duration. State which patients and settings are not represented.

For ophthalmology, check unit-of-analysis issues such as eye versus patient, inter-eye correlation, unilateral versus bilateral eligibility, handling of fellow-eye treatment, masking of image graders, imaging-device and reading-center consistency, learning effects, and whether anatomical surrogate changes correspond to visual function or patient benefit.

## Reviewer challenge

Identify the strongest alternative explanation for the observed result and at least one plausible counterexample when warranted. Frame reviewer questions so that each names the threatened inference and the evidence needed to resolve it. Do not inflate a list with cosmetic editorial comments.

Test the novelty claim against closely related prior work: distinguish a new mechanism or causal insight from a new population, endpoint, dataset, formulation, dosing schedule, analysis technique, or incremental replication. State whether the novelty is conceptual, methodological, clinical, or operational.

## MSL and lifecycle lens

Evaluate whether the paper can:

- close an evidence gap for an existing scientific narrative;
- motivate confirmatory evidence in a new population, disease stage, indication, combination, dose, formulation, route, endpoint, or care pathway;
- support investigator-initiated research or evidence-generation planning;
- refine unmet-need, patient-selection, adherence, safety, or outcomes questions;
- inform compliant scientific exchange.

Label these as evidence opportunities, research hypotheses, or validated findings. Never imply regulatory approval, comparative superiority, safety, or promotional suitability beyond the actual evidence and applicable label. Record when product context is insufficient to judge.

## Ratings

Use `高`, `中`, or `低`; do not compute a numerical composite.

- **证据可信度:** internal validity, precision, consistency, reporting completeness, and risk of bias.
- **临床相关性:** importance and directness of population, comparator, endpoint, magnitude, duration, safety, and applicability.
- **产品生命周期价值:** fit with the user's product context and the scientific plausibility, distinctiveness, feasibility, and evidence gap of a lifecycle opportunity. If product context is missing, mark `待补充背景`, not a guessed rating.
- **精读优先级:** combines decision relevance, evidence strength, novelty, unresolved uncertainty, and likely value of examining full methods, appendices, or cited studies.

Every rating needs a one-sentence rationale naming the dominant reason. A high rating in one dimension must not automatically raise another.
